⛳ OneShot Privacy Terms

Privacy Policy

Effective Date: August 14, 2026  |  Last Updated: August 14, 2026

This Privacy Policy describes how OneShot, a game by Stratum Works ("we," "us," or "our"), handles information. OneShot is a golf game for iPhone: one hole, one attempt, two controls.

The short version: We store an anonymous identifier from Apple, a display name you pick, and the shots you take. There are no ads, no tracking, no analytics companies, and no data brokers.

1. Signing in

Sign in with Apple is the only sign-in method. There is no password and no email address to remember, and nothing for anyone to leak.

When you sign in, we store:

  • An anonymous identifier from Apple. Apple gives each app a unique, opaque ID for your Apple Account. It is meaningless outside OneShot and cannot be used to identify you anywhere else.
  • A display name you choose, shown alongside your scores.
  • A session token, so you stay signed in. We store only a cryptographic hash of it, never the token itself.

What we deliberately do not store

  • Your email address. Apple offers it; we do not ask for it and do not keep it.
  • Your surname. We do not use it.
  • Your contacts, photos, location, health data, or microphone. OneShot never asks for any of these.

2. Gameplay data

OneShot's physics run on our server rather than on your phone — the app sends your aim and power and draws the result that comes back. As a consequence we store, for each attempt:

  • The course, the attempt number, and the angle and power you chose
  • The outcome, and whether the ball was sunk
  • The time of the attempt
  • A daily record of attempts used, so daily limits work

This is what makes a shared leaderboard meaningful: every score was produced by the same simulation, and can be replayed to confirm it.

3. Scores shown to others

If you appear on a leaderboard, the display name you chose and your score are visible to other players. Nothing else about you is shown. Choose a nickname if you would rather not be identifiable.

4. Advertising

OneShot currently contains no advertising. The game includes an option to watch a rewarded video for an extra attempt; at present this is a placeholder that grants the reward without showing anything, and no advertising software is included in the app.

If we ever introduce real advertising, we will update this policy before doing so and disclose exactly which provider is involved and what they receive.

5. What we do not do

OneShot contains no third-party analytics, no tracking SDKs, and no social media integrations. We do not sell, rent, or share your information with anyone. We do not use the Advertising Identifier (IDFA), and OneShot will never show you the App Tracking Transparency prompt, because there is no tracking to ask about.

6. Where your data lives

Account data and shot records are stored in a PostgreSQL database operated by Stratum Works on Railway, a hosting provider located in the United States. Data is encrypted in transit. Railway processes this data solely to host our service and does not use it for its own purposes.

Apple processes your sign-in. See Apple's Privacy Policy.

These are the only two third parties involved. There are no others.

7. Data retention

  • Account data is kept until you delete your account.
  • Shot records are kept while your account exists, and are deleted with it.
  • Sessions expire on their own and end when you sign out or delete your account.

8. Deleting your account

You can delete your account from within the app. This is permanent: your name, your sessions, your shots and your daily records are erased.

You may also revoke OneShot's access from your Apple Account at any time: Settings → your name → Sign-In & Security → Sign in with Apple → OneShot.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, export, or delete your personal information, and to object to certain processing. Deletion is built into the app. For anything else, email us and we will help.

We do not sell personal information as that term is defined under United States state privacy laws, and we have no mechanism to do so.

10. Children's privacy

OneShot is suitable for general audiences. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will delete it.

11. Changes to this policy

We may update this Privacy Policy. Changes will be posted here with an updated date. If a change is significant — particularly if we ever begin collecting something new, or introduce advertising — we will say so in the app rather than only here.
